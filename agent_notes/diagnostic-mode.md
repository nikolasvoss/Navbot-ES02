# Safe diagnostic mode: board observation, 2026-09-27

## Balance activation observation, 2026-09-27

- The user reported that after the normal firmware was restored and calibration saved, moving RC channel 5 to position 1 made the servos vibrate briefly, then the radio signal indication fell to zero and the motors stopped.
- The serial log printed `Voltage:7.38247` immediately before output ceased. In `RXsbus()`, this line is emitted when the filtered battery estimate is at most 7.4 V; the line that would disable the PID mode is commented out. It is therefore a warning, not a firmware shutdown command.
- Earlier diagnostic captures showed about 7.83 V with the drives inactive. A load-dependent voltage drop, control-loop oscillation, or both remain plausible. The log did not establish the 3.3 V rail voltage or a brownout reset reason. Capture servo commands, motor targets, and the minimum raw battery ADC value together before changing control gains.
- A later `balance-trace.log` captured CH5 changing from 0 to 1 around `time_ms=13792`. Before activation, filtered battery was about 7.76 V, the four servo commands were fixed at 15/14/11/16 degrees with zero 20 ms range, and both motor targets were zero. Within about 21 ms of activation, one complete row reported a minimum raw battery estimate of 5.263 V, servo command ranges of 6–8 degrees, and motor targets near -69/-74. Later complete rows reported minimum raw values down to 4.595 V, filtered voltage down to 6.289 V, servo command ranges up to 43 degrees in 20 ms, and motor targets reaching about +130/+200. Logging ceased about 144 ms after activation. Some serial lines were corrupted at 2,000,000 baud, but the trend appears in multiple complete rows.
- Practical consequence: activation coincides with large, rapidly varying actuator commands and a severe board-measured supply sag. The trace establishes both effects but does not resolve which starts the feedback cascade. Check battery, connectors, and board supply under load before changing gains; a controlled test with CH6 in assembly mode could later separate servo motion from wheel drive if the supply is sound.
- Static control-path review found that CH5 mode 1 selects the no-touch balance gains (`AnglePid.P=66`, `I=222`, `D=1`). `PIDcontroller_posture()` runs after an approximately 1 ms tick and computes an unfiltered derivative from `Angle_Pid.previousError`, initially zero. With the observed pre-activation `roll_ok≈-0.4°`, the first derivative term alone could demand roughly -400 velocity units at a 1 ms step. The SimpleFOC velocity path accepts the target directly, and the trace recorded targets around ±200 although `motor.velocity_limit` is 88. This is a deterministic startup demand and missing bound in the control path; it does not yet prove that these alone cause the shutdown.
- A local, unflashed control change now primes the balance derivative on each CH5 off-to-on transition and clamps both motor velocity targets to their configured `velocity_limit`. Wheel velocity estimation also divides each roughly 1 ms encoder delta by a hard-coded 10 ms; that scaling should be reviewed separately because correcting it changes the loop gain by about 10×.
- The user clarified that the robot was resting on a support with the wheels spinning freely during these traces. Thus the balance loop could not change the chassis attitude through wheel-ground contact. The large sustained motor and servo oscillations in this setup cannot by themselves establish unstable gains for ordinary standing. A refreshed trace on the same support showed the first complete CH5=1 row at `time_ms=13628` with `roll_ok=-0.38°`, motor targets about -248/-248, servo range only 1–2°, and raw battery estimate 7.174 V versus 7.77 V before activation. Later rows show motor target magnitude up to 424 and minimum raw battery estimate down to 4.681 V. The immediate -248 target remains consistent with a derivative kick independent of the missing ground contact; subsequent oscillations may be a consequence of this unsupported test geometry.
- A subsequent `balance-trace.log` was captured with both wheels on the ground. Before CH5 activation, filtered battery was about 7.77 V, servo commands were fixed at 15/14/11/16 degrees, and motor targets were zero. At `time_ms=18160`, the first complete CH5=1 row reported raw minimum 6.973 V and motor targets +75.5/+80.0. Around `time_ms=18201`, raw minimum was 4.805 V; later partially corrupted rows showed roll excursions around -6.7° to +5.5°, motor targets exceeding 400, and filtered voltage falling to about 6.0 V by `time_ms=18361` (raw minimum 4.537 V). Thus free-spinning wheels do not explain the entire failure. Values beyond ±88 show that the local derivative-prime/target-clamp change had not been flashed for this capture. The relative contribution of bad gain/derivative behavior and weak supply remains unresolved; do not tune from the free-wheel trace alone.
- The newest `balance-trace.log` (mtime 14:48) shows the user has since flashed the derivative-prime/target-clamp build: all readable CH5=1 motor targets stay within ±88. Before CH5, battery estimates are about 7.77 V, both motor targets zero, and servo commands fixed. The first active row at `time_ms=17637` is truncated but shows `roll_ok=-0.67°`, motor1 target about -44.5, servo ranges 1° and raw minimum 7.241 V. A pure proportional term of 66 × -0.67° predicts about -44, consistent with successful derivative priming. At `time_ms=17658`, both targets are +88, servo ranges 10–11° and raw minimum 5.559 V. By `time_ms=17879`, raw minimum is 4.499 V, filtered estimate 5.960 V, and logging stops. The guard did not prevent the failure. This does not distinguish excessive closed-loop demand from inadequate battery/connector delivery; the board ADC does not report the 3.3 V rail or reset reason. Avoid another full-power balance attempt until supply under load is checked.
- The user subsequently measured the voltage directly at the battery-pack output with an oscilloscope during balance activation and reports that it also collapses there. This independent measurement rules out a purely board-ADC artifact or a voltage drop confined to the downstream robot connector. The reported result does not yet include the minimum voltage, waveform duration, or a load-current measurement. Remaining possibilities include weak/discharged cells or pack contacts/protection, and excessive robot current draw; the trace alone cannot distinguish them. Avoid further full-power balance starts until those are separated.
- For the next diagnostic step, the user wants to assume hardware functionality because the robot operated two days earlier. The firmware provides an immediate gain comparison: RC CH5 mode 1 and mode 2 both execute `PIDcontroller_posture()`, but mode 1 uses no-touch angle gains P=66, I=222, D=1 and speed P/I=0.3/0.3, whereas mode 2 uses with-touch angle gains P=9, I=222, D=0.11 and speed P/I=0.12/0.12. In mode 1 a -0.67° angle error alone predicts a -44 motor velocity target; D=1 converts a 0.1° per 1 ms error change to another 100 target units, already beyond the ±88 diagnostic clamp. A brief, supported CH5=2 trace with neutral controls can test whether the high mode-1 gains drive the oscillation without another firmware upload. Neither this source comparison nor prior traces proves the exact gain that was present in the firmware that worked two days earlier.
- The user then captured `balance-trace-ch5-2.log`. CH5=2 persisted for about 2.35 s (`time_ms=15325..17672`). An early raw minimum was 5.779 V, then complete later rows had raw minima around 7.0 V and filtered voltage around 7.1 V; servo command ranges were small. However all 32 complete CH5=2 motor rows were saturated at opposite signs (±88), so mode 2 is not verified as stable balance control. On the return transition, the final ~0.34 s of logged rows were CH5=1, with no subsequent mode 0; raw minimum fell to 4.556 V, filtered to 5.760 V, and logging ended. This links the severe sag in this run to the mode-1 segment but does not isolate a single gain, since the two modes differ in speed gains and touch behavior, the initial attitude changed, and the motor angle/yaw contributions were not logged. See [balance-problem-handoff.md](balance-problem-handoff.md) for the neutral evidence summary.

## Observation and evidence

- The connected USB adapter appeared as `/dev/serial/by-id/usb-1a86_USB_Serial-if00-port0` -> `/dev/ttyUSB0`. An `esptool.py chip_id` probe reported ESP32-S3 revision v0.2. Recheck this before any later upload; port names can change.
- `OllieFOCdrive.ino` currently has `SENSOR_DIAGNOSTIC_MODE=0`. Setting it to `1` runs the earlier sensor-only bring-up: setup holds the two plain SimpleFOC driver enable pins (GPIO 16 and 37) low and returns before servo, driver, FOC, BLE and balance initialization. The active diagnostic loop only reads the IMU, battery ADC and SBUS receiver.
- The first verified upload succeeded, but serial text at 2,000,000 baud contained missing and corrupted fields. After changing only diagnostic Serial to 115200 baud, a verified upload produced 32 complete `DIAG` lines in about seven seconds after a reset.
- A plain `pyserial.Serial(port, baud)` open produced no lines: its asserted RTS held this board in reset. Opening with DTR and RTS set false before `open()` released it and produced a clean boot plus diagnostic stream. The earlier "no output" observation was caused by serial control lines, not an IMU failure. Opening the port still caused a brief reset in two subsequent captures, so reset-reason investigations require leaving the monitor open.
- A 20-second capture contained 97 complete data lines, one boot at monitor opening, no additional reboot, and receiver frame age at most 9 ms. The last line showed 7.830 V, ADC 820, and `rc_failsafe=0`. Stationary gyro readings remained around +0.019/-0.013/+0.026 rad/s; the uncalibrated attitude estimate drifted over the capture. These values are observations, not a validated balance tuning.
- Observed after filter settling: `imu_ok=1`, battery about 7.86 V, `rc_failsafe=0`, `rc_age_ms=9`, `ch3=992`, `ch5=192`; gyro, acceleration, orientation and temperature were present. The boot line reported `reset_reason=1` after a deliberate RTS reset.

## Practical consequence

Use the serial MCP server at 115200 baud and keep DTR and RTS low for plain-text output, or connect SerialPlot directly with both lines off. The earlier diagnostic image's filtered voltage needed about ten seconds to settle; the current CSV image reports direct ADC voltage immediately. The measurements establish that sensors and receiver can be read while drives are inactive; they do not identify why the robot previously shook or lost power under motor load. A later brownout would show `reset_reason=9`, and a reboot restarts the uptime channel. Check supply wiring and voltage under load before re-enabling motor control.

## SerialPlot update, 2026-09-27

- The diagnostic stream now emits 14 fixed-position numeric CSV columns every 50 ms. README.md lists their names and units. The battery column uses direct ADC conversion to avoid the filtered startup transient.
- Rebuilt for `esp32:esp32:esp32s3:USBMode=hwcdc,CDCOnBoot=default` and uploaded to the re-identified ESP32-S3 at `/dev/ttyUSB0`; every flashed segment passed hash verification.
- A seven-second hardware capture yielded 125 complete numeric rows, each with exactly 14 fields and a 50 ms median timestamp step. First row: raw battery 7.833 V, IMU ready; last row: raw battery 7.833 V, receiver age 3 ms, failsafe 0. Fifteen nonnumeric boot/setup lines preceded the stream, so set SerialPlot to a fixed 14-channel ASCII CSV format and leave DTR and RTS off.
- The user's SerialPlot 0.13.0 log showed `boot:0x21 (DOWNLOAD(USB/UART0))` and `waiting for download` after it opened `ttyUSB0`. This means the chip booted its flash loader, so it cannot emit firmware CSV. The likely trigger is DTR holding the ESP32's boot strap low during a reset; deassert both DTR and RTS in SerialPlot, then pulse RTS or press RESET/EN while DTR remains off. A normal boot reports `SPI_FAST_FLASH_BOOT`. The reported "invalid number of channels" warnings were for ROM boot text, not malformed diagnostic CSV.
