# Case C: Increasing tilt when driving fast

Status: open

As of 2026-09-27. This case file describes **only** the driving behavior reported later. The earlier standing oscillations and voltage dips are in [Case A/B](2026-09-27-balance-and-power.md); the separation of all observations is in the [problem map](2026-09-27-problem-map.md). A common technical trigger is possible but not established.

## Confirmed details of the test

- The user reported that with the most recently used soft diagnostic gains, the robot stands relatively stable on its own and responds to light pressure on either side with a matching driving movement. This observation after power-on again was not recorded as a separate trace. For the earlier standing traces and the gain history, see [Case A/B](2026-09-27-balance-and-power.md).
- When driving forward, the robot pulls slightly to the right according to the user. When driving backward, it often goes straight; the directional tendency is not reliably established.
- The user additionally reports: when he drives forward and stops the robot, it appears to overshoot. It sometimes drives backward again without a further control command, and occasionally tips over in the process. The exact stick positions, gain values, duration, and frequency of this behavior were not logged. This may be related to the increasing tilt at high speed, but it establishes neither the same cause nor a specific control-loop fault.
- During fast forward **and** backward driving via the speed stick (transmitter channel CH3, `sBus.channels[2]` in the code), the robot increasingly tilts in the direction of travel and speeds up. The user had to counter-steer with the remote control to prevent it from falling over.
- During this fast drive, CH8 was at position 2 (ball pose). The touchscreen is currently **not connected**, so no ball was on it either. The positions of CH7, CH9, and CH10 were not reported. For this drive there is **no serial trace**, no independent angle or speed measurement, and no voltage measurement.
- The slight oscillation at about 2 Hz described earlier at yaw P=10 was, according to a later clarification, a **forward/backward tilt about the wheel axle**, not a visible left/right turning of the direction of travel. This is an observation from a standing test and must not be equated with the increasing tilt at high speed.
- Note from the user: ch5=1 gave the same result.

## Static findings from the current local source code

- `RXsbus()` sets `MovementSpeed` from CH3 to the range ±15 and `BodyTurn` from CH4. CH8 determines `attitude_mode`; in position 2, the leg height is set by CH2. CH9 (`sBus.channels[8]`) produces `top_ball_x` in the range ±5, and CH10 the corresponding y value. This mapping says nothing yet about the actual stick positions during the drive.
- In `PIDcontroller_posture()`, the speed error is the averaged measured wheel speed minus `MovementSpeed`. `Speed_Pid.compute()` produces, via `BodyX`, a shift of the leg kinematics and thus the servo target angles. The wheel motor targets, by contrast, come from `Angle_Pid` and `Yaw_Pid` (`target1 = angleOutput - yawOutput`, `target2 = angleOutput + yawOutput`). In this **active two-wheel path**, the speed controller output does not enter the wheel motor angle target directly. Whether this kinematics is intended, and whether this path causes the reported behavior, is open. The older function `PIDcontroller_angle()` had a different coupling, but `loop()` did not call it. It was removed during cleanup on 2026-10-01; the active control path remains in place.
- At CH8=2, the active path computes `BodyPitching` from `Touch.XPdatF` and the smoothed CH9 value `sbus_top_ball_x_smoothed`, even when no touchscreen is connected. The filtered `BodyPitching_f` enters `angleError = roll_ok + BodyPitching_f` and thus the wheel motor setpoint. `Touch` is initialized globally and starts with zero values; without incoming data, `Touch.XPdatF` is presumably zero. Its actual value during the drive was **not** logged. The CH9 contribution stays active without a touchscreen.
- According to the code, the same `BodyPitching` calculation also runs at CH7/`roll_mode=AUTO`, even when CH8 is not in ball pose. CH8=0 alone therefore does not reliably separate the paths. In addition, `RXsbus()` does not explicitly set `BodyPitching` to zero at CH8=0; a value left in RAM from an earlier ball pose could distort a comparison.
- The wheel speed estimator divides the angle change per active control tick by a fixed `0.01f`, although the loop runs from `time_dt >= 0.001f`. The actual tick interval during the drive was not measured. At 1 ms, the reported wheel speed would be computationally ten times smaller than the actual one. With the most recently used CH5=2 gain preset, `Speed_Pid.Kp` and `Ki` are each 0.0012. `SpeedPid.limit=50` limits the integral state; its contribution to `BodyX` could computationally reach ±0.06. The actual `BodyX` value during the fast drive is unknown.

## What does **not** follow so far

- It is not established that the fast driving has the same trigger as the earlier voltage dips during CH5 switching. During the fast driving, neither pack voltage nor motor targets were logged.
- It is not established that a wrong-sign speed controller, a CH9 offset, touch data, servo movement, wheel speed estimation, or a supply effect **alone** causes the acceleration. The code findings provide testable hypotheses, not a determination of cause.
- The observed slight rightward drift when driving forward must be checked separately. Without driving and yaw measurements, it is not known whether it is related to the increasing tilt.
- The gain values actually flashed during the fast drive test were not read back at the same moment. Serial live gain changes are not stored permanently; a restart resets them to the values of the flashed image.
